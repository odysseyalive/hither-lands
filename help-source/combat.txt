{tile:capital:O|O}           f Arms and the Manner of Fighting
            =================================
            "That knife was made by Telchar of Nogrod...
            iron it would cleave as if it were green
            wood."
            -- The Silmarillion, ch. 19

There is a great deal of fighting in Beleriand and very little of it
is glorious.  The songs keep the duels -- Fingolfin at the gate,
Fingon on the height, Húrin standing over the last of his guard in
the Fen of Serech -- and they leave out the long ugly middle of every
battle, which is where you will be spending your time.  The Enemy
does not fight to be remembered.  He fights with numbers, and with
poison, and with things that were men once, and he can afford to
lose.

So fight the way the survivors of this Age fought.  Know what is in
front of you before it is on top of you.  Keep a road at your back.
Be faster than the thing you are killing, and where you cannot be
faster, be somewhere else.  Everything below is only the detail of
that.

Melee
-----

Most of it comes down to standing next to something and hitting it
until one of you stops.  Walk into a foe and you strike it.  Bare
hands are a poor argument in Beleriand, so bear a weapon and take it
up with '{key:Wear/wield an item}'.

Each blow rolls your skill against the target's armour, and a miss is
no damage at all.  Weapon damage is its dice plus enchantment, and
your blows per round grow with STR and DEX and shrink with the
weapon's weight -- warriors reach six blows, pure casters four, the
rest five.  Rings and other worn gear add their to-hit and to-dam to
your melee strikes.

The old quarrel between the heavy blade and the quick one is settled
differently for every arm that lifts them.  A weapon you swing twice
is generally worth more than a weapon you swing once and harder,
unless the once is enough, and against certain things it is.  Carry
both a while and let the corpses decide.

Shooting and throwing
---------------------

The better part of any fight in these lands is over before the enemy
arrives.  Beleg Cúthalion killed at a distance for thirty years and
was scarcely ever seen doing it, and a bow remains the only argument
that lets you win without being hit.

Fire ammunition with '{key:Fire your missile weapon}', or loose at
the nearest foe without aiming -- Appendix I gives that key for your
keyset.  Throw things with '{key:Throw an item}'.  Only a matching
launcher gives ammunition its full power, for the bow's multiplier
scales the whole shot.  Choose your mark with
'{key:Target monster or location}', or take the closest foe with
'{key:Target closest monster}'.

Arrows run out, and there is no fletcher under the mountains.  Gather
what you have shot once the floor is quiet, keep something plain for
the crowds, and save the good ammunition for whatever the crowd is
standing in front of.

Armour and resistance
---------------------

Mail is a slow answer to a fast problem, and it is still the best
answer anybody in the Elder Days has found.  The Noldor did not go to
war in cloth.  Neither should you, and neither should you imagine
that iron alone will see you through, because most of what wants you
dead down there does not intend to use iron on you at all.

Armour class turns blows aside and softens what lands, though heavy
body armour costs a little accuracy.  Elemental resistances are
percentages that stack multiplicatively, and temporary resistance
adds another layer.  Fire, cold, acid, lightning, poison, and
confusion are the six to secure first.  The elements also ruin
your goods -- fire eats scrolls, acid armour, cold potions,
lightning rings and wands -- so resistance guards your pack too.

A traveller who has been burned once tends to buy resistance the way
other people buy bread, and that is the right instinct.  Sooner or
later the cold gets past the mail, and then the only thing between
you and the floor is what you took the trouble to resist.

Perils of the deep
------------------

Deeper down, the fight stops being about weapons.  The old horrors of
this Age did not need to touch you to finish you.  Glaurung unmade an
army with his breath and a princess with his eyes, and the wights in
the barrows take something from a man that no leech can put back.
Learn these before you meet them, because meeting them is the
expensive way.

Dragon-breath takes a share of the beast's own vigour, less at a
distance, for there is no dodging it, only resisting.  The undead
drain your experience, and hold life is the true shield there.
Stunning spoils your aim and your spells; free action wards off
magical paralysis.  Speed is the king of stats, and ten points of it
doubles you.

Take that last one seriously.  Two of you against one of them is not
a metaphor, and every deep-dweller who ever came back up did so
because they were quicker than the thing behind them.

The advantage runs both ways, and the Enemy's creatures may be
afflicted in their turn.  Stun, confuse, slow, frighten or hold them,
and every such state opens them to heavier critical strikes.  A
blinded troll is barely a troll.  Cripple first and kill after, and
you will find that Beleriand is survivable, which is the most that
anyone in this Age was ever promised.
