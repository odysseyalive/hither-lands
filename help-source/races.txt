{tile:capital:O|O}           f the Peoples of the West
            =========================
            "Now the Children of Ilúvatar are Elves and Men, the Firstborn
            and the Followers."
            -- The Silmarillion, ch. 1
            Come down out of the passes of Ered Luin in the morning and
look west, and the first thing you will notice is the smoke.  It stands
in three or four thin columns along the northern sky and it never quite
goes out.  The second thing you will notice is the traffic.  The road
under the mountains is not empty, even now; it carries Dwarves with ore
and Dwarves with mail, Green-Elf hunters who will not speak to you,
Men driving thin cattle east away from the war, and now and then a
company of horse going the other way, north, in no hurry to arrive.

Beleriand is a small country with a great many nations in it.  In the
space of one season on the road you may drink with all of them, and be
turned from four doors, and be told four different accounts of who
began the trouble.  It is worth learning them apart before you need to
know whose gate you are hammering upon in the dark.

Thirteen peoples may take up the war against Morgoth, and no two of
them go at it alike.  Those marked (b) suit a traveller new to the
road; those marked (e) ask some seasoning.  Each keeps its own home
town in the wilderness game, and each will call you kin or stranger
accordingly.

The Elves
---------

The Firstborn hold most of what is still free, and they hold it
because they have been holding it a long while and mean to go on.  An
Elf remembers the war from its first day, which is a kind of strength
and also a kind of weight; they learn slowly what they do not already
know, having already learned so much.  Expect long silences, and be
careful how you speak of oaths.

  High-Elf (e)   - Noldor of Gondolin and Nargothrond, mightiest
       and most burdened; masters of every skill, they
       see the unseen from birth and start above first
       level, at the steepest experience cost.  They
       came back over the Sea for the jewels' sake and
       the Doom of Mandos came with them.

  Grey-Elf (e)   - Sindar of Thingol's Doriath; strong of arm and
       will, at home under trees, unharmed by light.
       They never went into the West and never wanted
       to, and they were fighting orcs under starlight
       while the Noldor were still learning smithcraft.

  Green-Elf (e)  - Laiquendi of Ossiriand; hunters and singers,
       keen-eyed, silent, deadly with bows, slight in
       close battle.  They wear green and go barefoot
       and you will not see one until it wishes it, and
       they have long memories for slaughter.

  Dark-Elf       - the Moriquendi who never saw the Light; the
       stealthiest of all folk, but frail, and the sun
       pains them.  To travel with one is to travel by
       night and rest at noon, and never once to be the
       first thing an enemy notices.

Take an Elf and you take the long view of this war, for good and for
ill.  The Naugrim in the mountains east take a shorter one, and get
rather more done before dinner.

The Dwarves and the Halflings
-----------------------------

The Dwarf-road runs from Belegost and Nogrod under Mount Dolmed and
down into Beleriand, and it is the busiest road in the country.  The
Naugrim came to this war as smiths and stayed as soldiers, and they
have never much cared which Elf-king is presently offended.  They are
plain dealers, hard bargainers, and the only folk here who can walk
into the deep dark without their nerve going.  With them are counted
the halflings, who arrived out of nowhere and no age of this one, and
whom nobody has yet had the heart to send back.

  Dwarf          - the Broadbeams of Belegost; strong, tough,
       never blinded, resistant to sorcery -- and
       loud as a forge underfoot.  Their lord Azaghâl
       put a knife in Glaurung and died of it, and they
       will tell you so within the hour.

  Longbeard      - Durin's Folk of Khazad-dûm, greatest of the
       Seven Houses; all a Dwarf's strengths written
       larger.  Their mansion east of the mountains is
       in its morning-glory, and they walk as folk who
       have somewhere better to go home to.

  Petty-Dwarf    - the Noegyth Nibin, Mîm's hunted kindred; the
       finest handlers of magical devices alive, free
       of movement, and easily broken.  They were
       hunted for sport before the Elves knew what they
       were, so they are small, quick, and slow to
       forgive; a life of hiding teaches the hands
       everything and the body nothing.

  Hobbit (b)     - halflings out of a later age; peerless with
       slings, locks, and shadows, hardy against
       sickness, feeble in a stand-up fight.  Nobody
       can say how they came here.  They do not seem
       greatly troubled by the question.

The Naugrim will fight beside you and go home again.  The Followers
have nowhere else to go, which is a different sort of courage.

The Men and the Others
----------------------

Men came late over the mountains, in the Sun's first years, and found
the war already old.  They are given seventy years or so and they
spend them fast.  Some entered the service of the Eldar and were
loved; some entered the service of the Enemy and were paid; most only
wanted a valley with no orcs in it, and did not get one.  With them
here are counted the stranger kindreds, the skin-changers and the
tree-shepherds who are older than any of us and in no hurry to say so.

  Easterling (b) - Men out of the East, the plain measure of
       mortal kind; sturdy, adaptable, any calling.
       Their name is under a shadow in these lands and
       will be under a darker one before the Age ends,
       which is a poor inheritance for a good soldier.

  Adan (e)       - the Edain of the Three Houses, friends of the
       Eldar; surpassing other Men in every field, and
       their endurance cannot be worn down.  Hador,
       Bëor and Haleth brought their people west into
       the fire, and their grandchildren are still in
       it.

  Drúadan (e)    - the Drúedain, allies of the Haladin; wise,
       quiet, tough, seeing minds from afar, though
       towns mistrust them.  They sit still for hours
       and are thought slow; they know where the orcs
       are, and are thought uncanny for it.  Both
       judgements are made by folk who do not listen.

  Beorning (b)   - skin-changers of the Anduin vales; hardy
       woodsfolk who may take bear-shape with the
       '{key:Change shape}' command.  East of the
       mountains they keep bees and mistrust strangers,
       and it is thought unwise to be a guest of theirs
       past sundown.

  Ent            - the Onodrim, shepherds of trees; terribly
       strong, slow-handed, and wary of fire above all
       things.  Nothing in Beleriand hits harder or
       thinks longer, and nothing else on this list has
       watched a forest grow from seed and then watched
       it burned.

Choose as you will.  The stat tables, the experience costs, and the
full particulars are set out on the race screen at birth as you
scroll.  What that screen cannot tell you is which door will open at
midnight when you knock, and that is the choice you are really making.
