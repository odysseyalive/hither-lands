{tile:capital:O|O}           f the Years
            ===========
            "...it was named Dagor Aglareb, the Glorious Battle.  A victory
            it was, and yet a warning..."
            -- The Silmarillion, ch. 13
            You have come in at the end of a very long argument.  It began

before the Sun and the Moon were made, over three jewels, and it has
since eaten two Trees, four kingdoms, and most of the Elves and Men who
ever lived in this country.  Nobody in Beleriand will explain it to you
from the beginning.  They assume you know, and they are tired.

The game is set in the First Age of the world, in Beleriand, the land
that lay west of the Blue Mountains.  As you go down, you go back
through the Age's own history.  Green realms and guarded kingdoms
above, ash and iron below, and at the bottom the beginning of all the
trouble.  The descent is a reading of the Age backwards.  The deeper
you stand, the earlier the wound.

The Age in brief
----------------

Here is the argument in nine steps, so that when a Grey-Elf spits at
the name of Fëanor over the wine, you will know why, and can change
the subject.

  1. The Elves awake at Cuiviénen and journey west; the
     Green-Elves turn aside and stay in Ossiriand, and are
     thought lesser for it by folk who later lost everything.

  2. Fëanor makes the Silmarils, and puts into three stones a
     light that cannot be made twice.  Morgoth and Ungoliant
     destroy the Two Trees and steal them.

  3. The Noldor depart under the Doom of Mandos to make war for
     the jewels' recovery.  The Doom is not a curse laid on
     strangers; it is a promise made to them, and it is kept.

  4. The Siege of Angband, the long peace in which Doriath,
     Nargothrond, and hidden Gondolin flower.  Four hundred
     years of quiet, and everyone alive believed it would hold.

  5. Dagor Bragollach, the Battle of Sudden Flame, in which
     rivers of fire break the Siege.  Fingolfin rides to the
     gate and dies.
     He rides alone, in wrath, and no one goes after him.

  6. The deeds of Beren and of Túrin, the Quest of the Silmaril,
     the fall of Nargothrond, the black sword's doom.  One tale
     ends in a marriage, the other in a river, and both are told
     wherever there is a fire and drink enough.

  7. Nírnaeth Arnoediad, the Unnumbered Tears, when treason
     broke the great alliance of the free peoples.  After this
     there are no more alliances, only holdouts.

  8. Gondolin falls, betrayed from within, and the last hidden
     kingdom goes up in smoke that the northern sky still
     carries.

  9. The War of Wrath, in which the Enemy is thrown down at
     last -- and that is where you come in.

So runs the Age, in brief.  Now stand at the top of the stair and take
it the other way.

The road down
-------------

Every dungeon is somebody's ruin, and the deeper ones are the older
grief.  You will not merely read about the fall of these places; you
will walk in on the fall itself, still going on, with the same enemy
still standing in the same hall.

Five appointed foes mark the descent, and each is a chapter of the
tale.  Mîm at Amon Rûdh, Glaurung at Nargothrond, Ungoliant in Nan
Dungortheb, Sauron at Tol-in-Gaurhoth, and Morgoth himself at the
hundredth depth of Angband.  Defeat the last and the Age is mended.

What each of their strongholds was in its own day is told in the
chapter of places, and it is worth reading before you go down, if
only so that you know what you are standing in when the floor of it
runs with water and you cannot say why.
