{tile:capital:O|O}           f Delvings, and How to Walk in Them
            ===================================
            Beneath every dungeon-marked hill and fortress of the map lie
            levels without end.  The first stair is usually a decent one,
            cut square, with the marks of the chisel still on it, and you
            go down it thinking well of whoever made it.  Four turns later

the cutting stops being square.  Somewhere below that it stops being cutting
at all and becomes something that was dug by hand, or by claw, and the air
changes, and your torch begins to lean toward the draught instead of
standing straight.

Such is a delving, repeated as often as your nerve
holds.  Stand upon a down-stair and press
'{key:Go down staircase}' to descend; '{key:Go up staircase}'
climbs back toward the light.  A level once left is gone forever, for the
deeps are vaster than any map, so take what you need the first time.  Most
levels offer one stair up and two down.

Understand what that means before you are standing in it.  There is no
going back for the thing you left in the corner.  There is no second look
at the room you did not open.  The deeps do not keep still behind you, and
what you walk away from you have given away.

Light
-----

Everything else in this chapter assumes you can see, and that assumption is
the first one the dungeon takes off you.

Without light you are blind, and there is no reading, no casting, no
picking of locks, no seeing of traps.  Carry a torch or a lamp and
spare fuel with it, and refuel with '{key:Fuel your light source}'
before the dark surprises you.  Rooms may be lit or unlit, and the
deeper you go the darker they are.  Necromancers alone walk
lightless, by choice and by art.

A guttering torch four hundred feet down is a worse enemy than most of what
lives there, because most of what lives there can wait.  Buy the extra
flask.  It weighs almost nothing and it has never once been the thing that
killed anybody.

Traps and doors
---------------

Light shows you the room.  It does not show you the floor, and the floor is
where the trouble is kept.

Traps lie hidden until your wits uncover them, or until you blunder
into one; '^' marks those you know of.  The uncovering is passive,
for there is no search command.  Disarm with
'{key:Disarm a trap or chest}', and carefully.  Trap-detection magic
marks out a green zone of safety, but it is a snapshot only, and what
the monsters dig afterward it does not know.  Doors are '+' when shut
and ''' when open, and a secret door wears the look of plain granite
until you pass close by it.  Open with '{key:Open a door or a chest}'
and close with '{key:Close a door}'.

A shut door behind you is worth more than most spells.  Things that hunt by
smell lose the trail at a closed door, and things that hunt by sight lose
you entirely, and the few that can open doors will announce themselves
doing it, which is a courtesy of a kind.

Mining
------

Sooner or later the way you want goes through the rock, or the rock has
something in it that you want, and then you dig.

Quartz veins '*' hold the richest strikes, and magma '%' the lesser.
Dig as Appendix I directs, and the game will wield your best digger
unbidden.

Always carry one.  Rock can close behind you, and a wall is only a wall
until you own the tool that disagrees with it; a shovel has saved more
lives than many a sword, and it has never yet been cursed.

Reading a level
---------------

The dungeon tells you things, if you will stand still long enough to be
told.

On arrival the level tells you something of itself, a danger feeling
from "quiet and peaceful" to "omens of death", and, as you explore, a
promise of treasure from cobwebs to superb.  Rest with
'{key:Rest for a while}', survey with '{key:Full dungeon map}', and
scroll the map with '{key:Locate player on map}'.

Learn to trust those feelings and learn when to walk away from one.  A
level that promises nothing and threatens much is a level you owe nothing
to.  The stair down is always somewhere, and it is always cheaper than
pride.

At the last depth of Angband, past every terror of the descent,
Morgoth himself bars the way down.  There is no level beneath him.
There does not need to be.
