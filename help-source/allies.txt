{tile:capital:O|O}           f Companions on the Road
            ========================
            "...he swore an oath of abiding friendship and aid in every need
            to Barahir and all his kin."
            -- The Silmarillion, ch. 18
            Not all that lives in Beleriand wishes you ill.

Go far enough and you will meet something that does not lift its head as
you pass.  The world is not divided cleanly into the Enemy and yourself.
Between the two there is a great deal of Beleriand still going about its
own business, and some of that business can be spoken to.

Consider what it is like.  You are two days into Nan Dungortheb with a
torch you do not trust, and the webs overhead are thick enough to carry
the light sideways.  Somewhere behind you a wolf has been keeping pace.
You have known it for an hour.  What decides whether that is the worst
hour of your life or the beginning of a friendship is one question, and
it is not a question about your sword.  It is a question about what the
wolf is, and there is a way to ask.

Reading a stranger
------------------

The wise look first.  Press '{key:Look around}' and the cursor walks
from creature to creature, naming each, and beside the name it sets
down the creature's temper in a single word.  There are four such
words, and they carry most of what this chapter has to teach.

  aggressive   it means you harm.  "(unaware)" means it has not yet
   noticed you, which is a gift, and gifts of that kind do not keep.

  neutral      it lets you be unless provoked.  Territorial animals
   are marked "territorial"; give them room and they will give you
   yours.

  aligned      it is fighting your enemies, or follows you.  Enemies
   of your enemy are common in this Age and cost nothing.

  following    it is one of your own followers, and will come when
   you go.

The monster list, '{key:Display visible monster list}', is the same
knowledge gathered into one place, and it marks your helpers with an
"(allied)" tag so you can count heads before opening a door.  In ASCII
mode the colours speak as well.  Neutral folk of the Free Peoples are
tinted green, and bright green when they fight for you; territorial
beasts are yellow, and other wild animals umber.  With graphics tiles
those tints do not apply, since a painted wolf is only a wolf, so let
the look command and the monster list answer for you instead.

The asking
----------

There is no eloquence in it, and no oratory.  The deed is called
"Recruit a monster", and in the standard keyset it answers to 'j'.
Press it, then a direction toward an ADJACENT neutral creature, and you
will have said whatever it is that gets said at such moments.

In the roguelike keyset 'j' walks south instead, so the command must be
reached another way.  Open the command menu with ENTER and find it
under "Hidden", or bind a keymap of your own.  Arrange it early
in a quiet corridor rather than a loud one.

What follows the asking is a contest, and these are its terms.

  Who may join -- neutral people of the Free Peoples, that is, the
   speaking races.  A character with the Beguile ability, which is a
   Druid specialty, may also charm wild animals that are not evil.

  The reckoning -- persuasion weighs your level against the
   creature's.  Great folk will not lightly follow the untried, and
   they are not wrong to weigh you.

  Giving offence -- fail against a much greater creature, more than
   five levels above you, and it takes the asking as an insult and
   turns hostile.  A milder failure leaves it merely unconvinced, and
   you may try again.

  How many -- you can lead 1 follower, plus one more at level 15 and
   another at level 30.  Three is as much company as Beleriand will
   allow any one traveller.

  The cost -- parley takes a game turn, whether or not it succeeds.
   In an empty wood that is nothing at all.  With something coming
   down the passage behind you it is a great deal.

Parting is done the same way it was begun.  Parley with a follower a
second time and it leaves off following you and returns to neutrality,
and there is no quarrel in it.  Beasts do not sulk.  Whether you ought
to feel anything about it is a matter between you and your conscience.

Walking with a wolf
-------------------

Allies need no orders, which is the first mercy.  You will not spend
your dungeon managing anybody.  A follower seeks out your enemies
within its hearing and fights them, and when no enemy is near it walks
with you, and there is no more to the arrangement than that.

What it changes is not chiefly the arithmetic of a fight.  It is the
corridor behind you.  A companion means one direction you no longer
have to watch, and in Nan Dungortheb that is worth more than armour.
You will find yourself listening for it.  You will find that when it
falls behind at a corner you stop and wait, which is not a thing you
did before you had one.

An ally never turns on you of its own will.  It may be killed, and
often is, for it does not know how deep you have brought it and would
not turn back if it did.  But strike or rob one and it will remember,
as the sons of Ulfang taught the Union of Maedhros that a false friend
is worse than an open foe.

The temper of the wild
----------------------

It helps to remember that most of what lives in Beleriand is not at war
with you.  Animals and growing things begin neutral rather than
hostile, and each kind has its own notion of what you are.

Territorial beasts attack if you press into their ground, and only if
you press into it; the ground is theirs and the pressing is yours.
Skittish ones flee, and go on fleeing, and running one down a long
passage is how travellers come to meet the things that live at the end
of long passages.  Molds and mushrooms strike only what stands beside
them, so the whole of that difficulty is solved by standing somewhere
else.  There is a great deal of Beleriand you may simply walk past.

Not all of it, of course.  Evil things, the undead, and the creatures of
Morgoth are enemies always, and no word of yours will alter that, and
the traveller who stops to reason with a wight has misunderstood the
wight.

A character with the Charm ability moves among beasts without alarming
them, which is less a power than a manner.  The rest of us make do with
courtesy, distance, and the sense to look before we strike.  Of the
beasts themselves, and how to know one kindred from another, see Of
Beasts and Fell Things.
