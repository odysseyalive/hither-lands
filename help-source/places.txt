{tile:capital:A|A}            Gazetteer of the Deep Places
            ============================
            "...encompassed within the Girdle of Melian."
            -- The Silmarillion, ch. 14
            The Hither Lands are Middle-earth itself, the mortal shore east
            of the Great Sea, and Beleriand is their war-torn west, where

this game is set.  What follows is what the wise remember of the places you
will walk, and the wise are not always right, and some of them are lying.

Take it as a guide is meant to be taken.  A name on a map is a promise that
somebody was there once and came back to say so.  It is not a promise that
you will.

The great cities
----------------

Four of the free peoples still keep halls worth the name in these years,
and a fifth is falling as you read.  You will not be welcome in all of
them, and you will not find the gate of one of them at all.

  Menegroth, the Thousand Caves

      Thingol's hall behind the Girdle of Melian, and the fairest
      dwelling east of the Sea.  The Dwarves of Belegost delved it and
      were paid in pearls out of the water, and they carved the pillars
      like beeches, with birds of stone in the branches and lamps hung
      among them.  Bright, and old, and closed.

  Gondolin, the Hidden Rock

      Turgon's white city in its ring of mountains, seven gates deep and
      not marked on any map that Morgoth has read.  The songs say it is
      found only by those it summons, and the songs are not wrong; the
      road that brought you there was laid for you before you took it.

  Belegost, the Great Fortress

      The mansion of the Broadbeam Dwarves under the Blue Mountains,
      where the mail is made that turns dragon-fire.  Their lord Azaghâl
      wounded Glaurung and was trodden down for it, and his people bore
      him out on their shoulders singing, and no enemy dared touch them
      on the road.

  Khazad-dûm

      The vast eastern mansion of Durin's Folk, in its morning-glory.
      Far off, untouched by this war, and rich beyond the telling of it.
      Nothing has yet been dug too deep there.  That comes later.

  Nargothrond

      Finrod's cavern-fortress above the Narog, hewn with dwarf-help
      until the halls ran a mile under the hill.  In these days it is
      already a name of dread, and the folk who live within a week's
      march of it have stopped saying it aloud.

In a city you may eat, and buy, and sleep with both eyes shut, and that is
the whole of what a city is for.  The places that follow are not for any of
those things.

The strongholds of the Enemy and the deep places
------------------------------------------------

These are the delvings the game will actually take you down into.  Every
one of them was something else before it was this.  That is worth holding
on to on the stairs, when the torch is guttering and the level below is
making a noise you cannot place.

  Angband, the Iron Prison

      Beneath Thangorodrim, the three peaks of slag that Morgoth heaped
      out of his own digging.  He sits crowned with stolen light at the
      bottom of it and has not left his chair since Fingolfin came alone
      to the gate and knocked.  The air on the last stair smells of hot
      iron.  There is nothing under him.

  Nan Dungortheb, the Valley of Dreadful Death

      A cleft under the Mountains of Terror where Ungoliant's brood
      still spins.  The water there is bad and the dark is worse, and
      the webs are strung across the way at the height of a man's face.
      Beren crossed it in his youth and would never afterward say what
      he had seen, and men who knew him said he came out grey.

  Tol-in-Gaurhoth, the Isle of Werewolves

      It was fair Tol Sirion once, and Finrod built a watchtower on it
      to hold the pass, and it was the pride of the north.  Sauron took
      it.  Now the howling carries downriver on still nights for miles,
      and the boatmen of the Vale will not tie up within sight of the
      island, and they are right not to.

  Amon Rûdh, the Bald Hill

      A red crown of seregon over the moorland, flowering like spilt
      blood in the late year.  Under it Mîm keeps the last halls of the
      Petty-Dwarves, who were driven out of the west long ago and have
      forgotten why they should love anyone.  It is the shallowest of
      the deep places, and it is where most travellers learn what a
      dungeon is.

Begin at Amon Rûdh.  Nearly everyone begins at Amon Rûdh.  The ones who
begin elsewhere are seldom written about, there being nobody left to do it.

The wild regions
----------------

Between the delvings lies the country itself, and the country has a temper.
Some of these names are places you will cross a dozen times without
trouble.  Others are the reason the roads bend where they do.

  Anfauglith, the Gasping Dust

      A green plain until the Sudden Flame came over it in a night and
      burned the grass to the bone.  Now it is ash and grit, and the
      wind off it tastes of the forge.  Nothing lives there that was not
      sent.

  Dorthonion

      The highland of pines where Barahir's outlaws held out after the
      country fell, twelve men and a dog's loyalty, and fell in their
      turn.  The trees are still standing.  Little else is.

  Doriath, the Fenced Land

      Girdled by enchantment, so that the paths bend back on themselves
      and the traveller walks a day and arrives where he started.  It is
      not malice.  It is simply not for you.

  Sirion Vale

      The pass and course of the great river, the highway of the Age.
      Every army that has ever mattered has gone up or down this valley,
      and most of them only once.

  Talath Dirnen, the Guarded Plain

      Watched from Nargothrond by scouts you will never see, who have
      counted you since you came over the last rise.  Behave yourself.

  Ossiriand, the Land of Seven Rivers

      Where the Green-Elves sing unseen among the alders, and the water
      is loud enough that you will hear the singing only when you stop
      walking.  The gentlest country in Beleriand, which is faint praise
      and still true.

  Fen of Serech

      A reed-marsh at the throat of the north, where rearguards buy time
      with blood.  They have done it more than once.  They will do it
      again before this is finished.

East over the Blue Mountains lie quieter lands -- Eriador, the vales
of Anduin, the Gladden Fields -- out of which the humbler peoples
come.  And some names upon the map belong to later songs than this
one; walk them gently, for they are visiting, as you are.
