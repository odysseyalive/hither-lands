{tile:capital:O|O}           f Coming into Beleriand
            =======================
            "By the starlit mere of Cuiviénen, Water of Awakening, they
            rose from the sleep of Ilúvatar..."
            -- The Silmarillion, ch. 3
            Every hero of the Elder Days began somewhere, and most of

them began badly, in a cold country, owning very little, with the
strong opinion that somebody ought to do something.  Beren came over
the mountains of Dorthonion alone and half-starved and made himself
into the most famous outlaw of the Age.  Tuor was a slave.  The tale
does not begin when you are ready; it begins when you cross the
border, and the border is where you are standing now.

Six screens make a traveller of you, and the arrow keys and ENTER
drive them all, in either keyset.  Take your time over them.  Most of
what they settle can never be settled again, and the game is old
enough and hard enough that it will not be settling any of it for you
out of pity.

The birth screens
-----------------

They come in order and each narrows the road a little.  The first two
are the great choices, the third is the one people regret, and the
last three are yours to enjoy.

  1. RACE    - choose among the thirteen peoples, of whom Of the
   Peoples of the West tells.  Stat changes and the
   experience penalty are shown as you scroll.  This
   decides your kin, your home gate, and who will
   shut a door in your face.

  2. CLASS   - choose a calling; of these see Of Callings and
   Crafts.  What the world made you is settled; this
   is the part you answer for.

  3. OPTIONS - birth options are locked for your character's whole
   life.  Two notable defaults: a starting kit of gear
   (in place of extra gold), and no-selling (shops pay
   nothing, but you find more gold).  Read them once
   with attention now, and you will not have to
   wonder about them for the next ten thousand turns.

  4. STATS   - the point-based buyer is the default and comes
   pre-filled with sound choices; unspent points
   become starting gold.  The standard roller rolls
   instead, and will roll again at your word until you
   accept.  The buyer is the wiser road, though there
   is an old pleasure in the rolling.

  5. HISTORY - edit your background story, if you wish.  It changes
   nothing and it is worth doing anyway; a character
   with a grandfather in it dies harder.

  6. NAME    - last of all, the name.  End it with a Roman numeral
   ("Fred I") and your next of that line will succeed
   it automatically, which is a quiet mercy in a game
   that kills as freely as this one.

The five stats
--------------

Two screens back you were asked to weigh these against one another
without being told what they do.  This is what they do.  Nothing here
is decoration; every one of the five is somebody's whole reason for
still being alive.

  STR - to-hit and damage, carried weight, and extra blows with
        heavier weapons.  The fighter's early friend.

  INT - spellcasting for mages, rogues, necromancers, and
        blackguards; also magical devices and disarming.

  WIS - spellcasting for priests, paladins, druids, and rangers;
        also your saving throws against magic.

  DEX - extra blows with light weapons, to-hit, dodging, locks,
        and holding onto your purse.

  CON - hit points and recovery.  Matters most in the deep places,
        where nothing comes at you once.

Hit points come of your race and class together; spell points, for
those who cast, grow with level and with the casting stat.  Warriors
never have spell points, and want none.  Whatever you are, the first
few levels are the thin ones, and more heroes of this Age have been
lost in the third week than in the third year.

Where you begin
---------------

The last screen fades and you are somewhere real, with a road going
out of it.

In the wilderness game each race begins in its own home town:
Grey-Elves in Menegroth, Dwarves in Belegost, and so on.  The folk of
the more perilous lands begin above first level, having been seasoned
by simply having grown up where they did.  In the
dungeon-only game all begin equal, at the gate of one deep hold, and
the world above is a rumour.

With the Hither Lands tileset your sprite is of your own race; its
gender is settled when the tileset is installed, not at birth.  Look
down at yourself once before you set out.  You will be looking at
that small figure for a long while, and if the Age goes as it has
gone for everyone else, not long enough.
