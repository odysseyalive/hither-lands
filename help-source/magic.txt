{tile:capital:O|O}           f Song and Power
            ================
            "For Felagund strove with Sauron in songs of power,
            and the power of the King was very great; but Sauron
            had the mastery."
            -- The Silmarillion, ch. 19
That is the shape of magic in the Elder Days.  Two beings stand in a
dark place and sing at one another, and the stronger song bends the
weaker, and the loser goes down.  There are no gestures here and no
smoke.  Power in this Age is sung, and prayed, and studied, and it
comes out of who you are and what you have been given rather than
out of any trick of the fingers.

Finrod Felagund lost that contest and it cost him his crown, his
company and his life, and he is still counted among the mighty for
having come as close as he did.  Bear that in mind on the days when
a song fails you.  The greatest singer in Beleriand was beaten by a
lieutenant, and it was a near thing.

Four realms of five books each serve the four kinds of singer, and
which realm is open to you was settled at your birth, when you chose
what you would be.

  Arcane (INT)  - the Mage's study, and a little of the Rogue's.

  Shadow (INT)  - the Necromancer's dark road; the Blackguard
      borrows its destructions.

  Holy (WIS)    - the Priest's prayers, granted by the Valar in
      their own order, not chosen; the Paladin prays
      more slowly and never reaches the highest.

  Nature (WIS)  - the Druid's verses; the Ranger sings the
      survival-songs of wood and trail.

Warriors carry no song and want none.  Pure singers use
all five books of their realm, and the hybrid callings only the
first few.  The hybrids are not lesser people for it; a Ranger who
can heal his own arm in the field has settled an argument the Mage
is still having with a book.

Singing
-------

A song must be learned before it can be sung, and no one has ever
learned one usefully while being eaten.  Everything in this section
is work you do in the quiet, so that the loud part goes better.

These pages speak of song, which is how the loremasters of the First
Age spoke of such power.  Your character sheet still reckons the
strength for it as SP, the old shorthand, but the art is the same.

Sing or pray with '{key:Sing a song}', browse a book with
'{key:Browse a book}', and learn new songs with
'{key:Learn new songs}' when your level allows.  A song takes a full
turn.  Failure starts common and grows rare with level, and the great
singers come to fail not at all.

Singing beyond your strength is possible, and it asks a price.
The attempt may leave you drained, or paralyzed mid-word.

Every art has its conditions, and no singer has ever talked one of
them round.  You cannot sing blind or confused, and every singer but
the Necromancer needs light to read by.  The Necromancer reads the
dark itself, and light is a wound to that art.

What you wear is one of those conditions.  Mail is a weight on the
singer.  Carry more armour than your calling was meant to bear and
the measure of song you can hold falls away, one for every ten units of
excess, and they come back the moment the weight is set down.  The
allowance is widest for the Blackguard, who was never expected to
travel light, and narrowest for the Mage and the Necromancer, whose
strength was never meant to be worn.  Priest, Druid, Rogue, Paladin
and Ranger stand between.  Those who take the Armor Proficiency
specialty are granted a little more room before the penalty begins.

Gloves are the one mercy.  In other worlds a singer's hands must go
bare; here they need not.  A glove is only the weight it is, and
nothing about it dulls a voice or a hand.

Devices
-------

There is a second kind of power, older in a sense, because it was put
into the thing by somebody else and only waits there for a hand.  A
wand does not care whether you were taught.  This is how the unlearned
of Beleriand fight fire, and there is no shame in it whatever.

Wands are aimed with '{key:Aim a wand}', staves used with
'{key:Use a staff}', rods zapped with '{key:Zap a rod}', and worn
things activated with '{key:Activate an object}'.  Devices obey
skill and not realm, and even a Warrior may lean on a good wand,
though it is the Mage who outshoots everyone with them in the end.

Which is the way of things.  The one who understands how the making
was done gets the most out of what was made, and that has been true
in these lands since the first forge was lit.
