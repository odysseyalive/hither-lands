{tile:capital:O|O}           f Beleriand and Its Realms
            ==========================
            "...there were mighty convulsions in the earth, and Beleriand
            was broken and laid waste..."
            -- The Silmarillion, ch. 24
            That breaking is not yet come upon the land.  Ered Luin stands

behind you with the snow still lying in its passes, and westward the country
falls away league upon league toward a Sea you cannot yet see.  There is a
whole world there to be walked, and no part of it is safe.

Look west from the last ridge and you will take in most of it at once.  The
green plains, and the forests dark as standing water, and the grey backs of
the mountains, and the rivers that come down out of them loud and cold.
Southward the land softens into reed and fen and the slow country of the
Seven Rivers.  Northward it hardens.  Beyond the last of the highlands the
grass gives out altogether and there is a dust there that nothing crosses
willingly, and past that, if the air is clear, three peaks of slag stand up
against the sky with smoke lying flat above them.  That is the end of the
road, though it is a long way off yet.

Under all of it the deep places are sunk where the old tales say they
stood, delvings of dwarves, of elven-kings, and of the Enemy.  No tale
agrees on how far down any of them goes.

Of the roads between
--------------------

You will spend more of your life on the road than in any hall, so learn the
road first.  Region is joined to region by paths, and a path is worked with
the same keys as a stair.  Stand upon one and press
'{key:Go up staircase}' toward the safer country, or
'{key:Go down staircase}' toward the danger, for depth is danger here
as it is below.  Run as Appendix I directs, step warily with
'{key:Walk}', and press '{key:Start exploring}' to be drawn on toward
what you have not yet seen.

There is no magic carriage and no horse that will stay with you.  You walk,
as Beren walked, and you carry what you eat.

By day the wild is lit, and by night you must carry your own light.
The beasts of the wilderness are sleeping then, so that the dark hours
are quieter than the bright ones and a good deal more frightening.
Towns keep their own hours, and their gates do not close, whatever the
songs say of prudent folk.

Of the four worlds
------------------

Beleriand is not offered to you in one shape only.  Four world-maps are
laid out at birth, and the choice you make there decides what kind of
journey you are agreeing to.

The standard and the extended wildernesses hang the dungeons from an
overworld of paths, so that the walking between is itself the tale.  The
hybrid game spares you the walking and strings the dungeons in sequence
through portals.  And the plain Angband game forgets Beleriand altogether
and keeps one town and one long stair down, with Sauron near the bottom of
it, and Morgoth at the last.

Whichever you take, five dungeons anchor the map.  Amon Rûdh of the
Petty-Dwarves stands first and least, a bald red hill above the moors.
Nargothrond is under the Guarded Plain, and Nan Dungortheb under the
Mountains of Terror, and Tol-in-Gaurhoth keeps the crossing of the Vale of
Sirion.  Angband is behind the Gasping Dust, and Angband is last.

Each of them keeps a guardian, and none of the guardians is reasonable.
Their tales are told in the Gazetteer, and it is worth reading the tale of
a place before you stand in its doorway rather than after.

Of coming home
--------------

Nobody walks all the way back.  A scroll of Word of Recall will snatch you
out of the wild or out of the deeps and set you down in your home town,
though not at once; and read at home it will carry you back down to the
furthest point of your daring.  Read a second scroll to change your mind.
Should you come to another town and like it better, press
'{key:Move house}' and your house moves there.

So the country lies.  You go out light, you come back heavy, and
each time the road you take out begins further down than the last.
