{tile:capital:O|O}           f the Great Treasures
            =====================
            "In that time were made those things that afterwards were most
            renowned of all the works of the Elves."
            -- The Silmarillion, ch. 7
            The great works of the Age lie hidden in Beleriand, waiting.

You have come late to the making.  Nearly every famous thing you may
lift out of the dark was hammered out before you were born, by hands
that are mostly dust now, for a purpose that was not yours.  There is
one of each, and there will never be another.  A great treasure is
not simply a better sword.  It is a piece of somebody else's story
that you have picked up, and it is heavier than it looks.

They do not announce themselves.  A nameless blade and a named one
sit much the same in the hand, and what you come to understand of
the second you will learn by carrying it into trouble.  What you have
understood so far, '{key:Examine an item}' will tell you back.  The
rest is in the songs, and the songs are not always kind.

Here are a few of the names you may meet below, and what is
remembered of them.

  Ringil
      The sword of Fingolfin, High King of the Noldor, that glittered
      like ice.  When his people had been broken on the Anfauglith he
      rode alone to the gates of Angband and knocked upon them, and
      called Morgoth out to single combat.  With Ringil he dealt the
      Enemy seven great wounds, and Morgoth has gone lame ever since.
      The High King did not come back.

  Angrist
      "Iron-cleaver," forged by Telchar of Nogrod, carried out of
      Nargothrond by Curufin and taken from him by Beren.  It cut a
      Silmaril free of the Iron Crown without complaint.  Then Beren,
      who never knew when a thing was finished, tried for a second
      stone, and the blade snapped, and a shard of it flew and struck
      the cheek of Morgoth, and Morgoth stirred in his sleep.

  Gurthang
      "Iron of Death," the black sword of Túrin Turambar, reforged
      out of Anglachel, which was made of iron that fell from heaven.
      It was cold, and it was malicious, and it spoke, and it did not
      forget what it had been asked to do.  At the end Túrin asked it
      whether it would drink his blood, and it answered that it
      would, and gladly.

  Anguirel
      Eöl the Dark Elf made two blades out of a fallen star.  One he
      gave away and one he kept, and this is the one he kept.  His
      son Maeglin stole it from him and bore it into Gondolin, and it
      was not the last thing Maeglin took that was not his.

  Aranrúth
      "King's Ire," the sword of Elu Thingol of Doriath, who was a
      king in these lands before there were any Noldor here to argue
      with him.  It came out of the ruin of Menegroth when very
      little else did, and went down the long years afterward to the
      hands of the Kings of Númenor.

  Glamdring and Orcrist
      Foe-hammer and Goblin-cleaver, made in Gondolin for the war and
      paired as brothers are paired.  They burn cold when orcs come
      near, which is a courtesy the smiths built into them on
      purpose.  Both of them outlast the city that made them.  Long
      after this Age has become a song, strangers who cannot read
      their runes will pull them out of a troll's cave.

  The Dragon-helm of Dor-Lómin
      The Helm of Hador, grey steel crowned with the likeness of
      Glaurung in gold, made by Telchar and handed down a line of
      lords who each in turn found it too heavy to wear.  Túrin wore
      it.  The servants of Morgoth fled the sight of it, which was
      the whole purpose of the thing.

  Belthronding
      The great black bow of Beleg Cúthalion, cut of yew, that no
      other archer ever bent.  Beleg was the truest friend Túrin had,
      and Túrin killed him in the dark by mistake, in the moment when
      Beleg was cutting him loose from his bonds.  The bow was buried
      with the archer.

  The Elessar
      The green Elfstone, in which a little of the light of the elder
      Sun was caught, so that withered things seen through it looked
      young again, and hurt things touched by the hand that held it
      were made whole.  It was wrought in Gondolin and given for
      love, and its fame runs clean out the far end of this Age.

  Nimphelos
      A pearl the size of a dove's egg, white as starlight, that
      Thingol paid to the Dwarves of Belegost for their labour in the
      delving of Menegroth.  Their lord held it worth more than a
      mountain of gold and said so aloud, which from a Dwarf is an
      extravagance.

  Camlost
      "Empty-handed."  Beren took that name for himself when he came
      back to Thingol's hall and lifted his arm and there was no hand
      on the end of it, and no Silmaril in the hand, Carcharoth
      having taken the two of them together.  The gauntlets remember
      what the hand held.

Not all of the great work of the Age was done in the light.  Weapons
and armour of the Enemy's making lie below also -- black things of
meteor-iron and worse, hammered out in Angband for hands unlike
yours, and they are not always the poorer for it.  Whether to wield
them is your own affair.  Many have.  A few were even glad of it
afterward.

It helps to know the hands that did the work, for you can often read
a thing's parentage off its surface.  The smiths of the Age worked in
ranks of matter: dwarf-iron grey and runed, elven steel blue-white,
meteor-iron black with a glint of stars, and rarest of all, work that
saw the Light of Valinor itself and kept some of it.

Carry such things well, and do not be too proud of them.  Every one
of them outlived the person it was made for, and every one of them
will outlive you.  None of them has ever saved anybody who was set on
being a hero.  They are only the best tools in the world.
