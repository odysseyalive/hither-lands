{tile:capital:O|O}           f Skin-Changing: the Full Particulars
            =====================================
            "...he took thence the ghastly wolf-hame of Draugluin, and the
            bat-fell of Thuringwethil."
            -- The Silmarillion, ch. 19
            *** SPOILERS ***  This file lists every shape with its numbers.

If you would rather discover them in play, close this file now.  Of
Skin-Changing tells a traveller all that is needed and no more, and there
is a real pleasure in the first time a fox turns out faster than you had
reckoned on.  What follows trades that pleasure for the ability to plan.
Choose knowingly.

*** SPOILERS BEGIN HERE ***

The notation is plain.  Hit/dam/AC are combat modifiers.  Named flags are
gained while shaped and go again when you revert.  Equipment worn keeps
all its own powers throughout, in every shape, without exception.

How each shape is gained
------------------------

No shape comes free.  Most are spells, and arrive with the book that
holds them, and the levels below are the levels at which the verse may
first be learned.

  Druid      - Fox Form L1 [Lesser Charms]; Become Pukel-man L30,
   Eagle's Flight L35, Bear Form L40 [Creature
   Dominion].

  Necromancer- Bat Form L3 [Into the Shadows]; Warg Form L28
   [Fear and Torment]; Vampire Form L37 [Corruption
   of Spirit].

  Blackguard - Werewolf Form L32 [Fear and Torment].

  Beorning   - bear at will with '{key:Change shape}', cub below
   L20, bear to L39, great bear from L40.

  Anyone     - dragon-scale mail's activation turns you into its
   dragon; certain rarer items grant the beast-forms.

The Druid's is the long road and it ends best.  The Necromancer buys
early and pays for it in blood.  The Blackguard is given one shape and
wants no other.  The Beorning simply grows into it, which is the only
recorded instance in Beleriand of a power arriving on schedule.

The beast-forms
---------------

Ten of them, and no two are meant for the same work.

  Fox          -3/-3/+3.  STR-3, Stealth+5, +1 blow, +1 move;
   feather fall, free action.  Bite and claw.

The fox is no fighting shape and never becomes one.  It is for going
somewhere quietly and arriving with your choices still in hand.

  Bear cub     +5/+5/+0.  STR+1, INT-3, infra+3; clumsy paws
   (disarming suffers).  Four light blows.

A cub strikes four times and thinks about half as well, and the paws
are honest about what they cannot manage.  It is a beginning.

  Bear         +10/+10/+2.  STR+2, INT-2, CON+1; fearless.
   Five blows.

Here the shape stops being a novelty.  Fearless means that you will not
run, which is either the making of you or the end of you.

  Great bear   +15/+15/+5.  STR+3, INT-2, CON+2, +1 blow;
   fearless.  Seven blows -- a mill of claws.

Seven blows is less a fight than a weather event.  You walk into a room
and the room is over.

  Eagle        +5/+0/+10.  +3 moves; flying, trap-immune, sees
   the unseen, cannot be blinded, confused, or
   frightened; free action.  Five rending blows.

The finest travelling shape in the game, and it is not a close contest.
Nothing on the floor can reach you and nothing in your head can either.

  Bat          +0/-10/+10.  Speed+3, Stealth+3, infra+5; flying,
   trap-immune, sees invisible, cannot be blinded.
   Entering costs a little blood.  Two weak blows.

The bat can hurt nothing and does not intend to.  It is a shape for
crossing a level that wants you dead while declining the argument.

  Warg         +5/+5/+0.  +2 blows; entering hurts briefly,
   cures fear, and rouses a berserk fury.  Four
   savage blows.

Putting on the warg is decided in the first second and considered
afterwards, or else not at all.  The fury is not a thing you choose.

  Vampire      +3/+3/-5.  Speed+5, Stealth+5, infra+5; sees
   invisible, holds life; your own light dims.
   Entering costs a quarter of your health; your
   bite drinks blood back.

The fastest shape here, bought at a price paid in advance out of your
own body, and repaid only if you win.  Your light dims because the
shape is what it is, and the dark it likes is not the dark you see in.

  Werewolf     +5/+5/+0.  +1 blow, +1 move; regenerating.
   Entering terrifies the nearby, cures fear, and
   rouses fury.  Four rending blows.

The room empties before ever you strike.  Sauron built a reputation on
this shape, and the reputation was fairly earned.

  Pukel-man    +0/+5/+20.  STR+4, CON+4, save+20, damage cut by
   10, poison-proof, shard-hard, Speed-5; sustained,
   regenerating, stun-proof, holds life; cures
   poison and stunning on entry.  Four stone fists.

Stone does not hurry and has no need to.  Nobody wears the Pukel-man to
chase anything.  It is worn to stand immovable in a doorway while the
rest of the level breaks itself upon you.

The dragons
-----------

Twelve more, and they are one beast wearing twelve different weathers.

All twelve dragon-shapes share one body: +0/+5/+10, Stealth-3,
devices+10, throwing-30, four heavy blows -- bite, bite, claw, tear.
Re-activate the armour WHILE in dragon-shape to breathe.  Each colour
keeps its own element, total in its kind:

  black acid  -  blue lightning  -  white frost  -  red fire  -
  green poison

and the high dragons besides: shining (light; sees invisible), law and
chaos (hold life), gold (sound; free action, unconfused), multi-hued,
balance, and power -- whose breaths are as their names.

The dragon is the loudest thing a traveller can do.  Stealth-3 and a
body the width of the corridor mean the level knows where you are, and
the throwing penalty is the game observing that a dragon has no earthly
use for a sling.  Breathing while shaped is the point of the whole
exercise, and forgetting that you may is the commonest waste in this
list.

Practical notes
---------------

A few things that the numbers alone will not tell you.

  A shape replaces your skills and blows wholesale.  A warrior's
   sword-arm is wasted in a fox, and a druid's fox wastes nothing.

  The entry costs of bat, warg, and vampire are paid out of your own
   body, so do not put on the vampire at a sliver of health.

  The Pukel-man trades speed for near-invulnerability, a shape for
   standing in doorways and not for chasing.

  Reverting is free; shifting in is a turn (Beorning) or a cast.

None of which is the same thing as knowing what to do.  That arrives
the first time you shift for the right reason at the right moment, and
the level that was going to kill you turns out to have been survivable
all along.

Every shape has its own sprite under the Hither Lands tileset, five of
them painted for this set alone.
