{tile:capital:O|O}           f Goods and Gear
            ================
            "The heart of Fëanor was fast
            bound to these things that he
            himself had made."
            -- The Silmarillion, ch. 7
Fëanor is the warning at the head of this chapter and not the model.
The greatest craftsman who ever lived bound his heart to three
jewels, and burned the ships, and dragged his whole people into a
ruin that is still going on around you.  Things are dangerous.  Not
because they are cursed, though some are, but because a traveller
who will not put a thing down when the time comes to run will be
found later, still holding it.

Still, no one ever walked into Angband empty-handed on purpose.  Let
us be practical about the carrying.

Carrying
--------

A traveller's pack in the Elder Days is a small and jealous country,
and everything you put into it you have chosen not to put something
else.  Walk onto an item to take it, or gather it with
'{key:Pick up objects}'.  The pack holds twenty-three kinds of
thing, each stacking to forty, and twelve more places are worn upon
your body.  Weight past your strength slows you.  Wear or wield with
'{key:Wear/wield an item}', take off with
'{key:Take off/unwield an item}', drop with '{key:Drop an item}', and
inscribe with '{key:Inscribe an object}', and your notes ride along
with the item.

Inscription is worth more than it looks.  The dungeon is long and
memory is short, and a word cut into the haft of a wand will still be
there in an hour, when you have forgotten what the wand was for and
something with too many legs is coming up the corridor.

Knowing what you have
---------------------

The hard part of this Age is that nothing tells you what it is.  The
loremasters who could name a thing on sight are behind you in
Menegroth and Nargothrond, and they are not coming down here.  What
you carry, you carry on trust, until it has proved itself one way or
the other.

Every potion, scroll, ring and amulet kind wears a random
disguise each game ("a copper ring") until it is learned.  There
is no scroll of Identify in this Age, and knowledge is earned.
Weapons and armour reveal their runes as you fight in them; devices
reveal themselves in use, so aim them at something easy first;
scrolls teach by reading, which is safest done standing on
stairs; potions and mushrooms teach the bold.  Shopkeepers know
their trade also, and selling an unknown thing reveals it.

Unknown properties show as {??}, and the game marks {cursed},
{tried}, and {empty} as it learns alongside you.

So the pack fills with half-known things, and each of them is a small
wager.  This is the ordinary condition of everyone who has ever gone
under a mountain looking for treasure, and there is no cure for it
except going under the mountain again.

Ignoring the dross
------------------

Rubbish accumulates faster than gold.  Every orc you kill leaves a
bent dagger behind it, and after the fortieth bent dagger the floor
stops meaning anything and you begin to walk past the things that
matter.

The ignore command puts an item, or its whole kind, out of your
sight, and '{key:Toggle ignoring of items}' shows or hides everything
so dismissed.  Appendix I gives the ignore key for your keyset.
Quality levels let you sweep all bad gear from sight at once.

Use it early and use it hard.  A clean floor is a floor you can read
at a glance, and reading the floor at a glance is occasionally the
difference between leaving a level and staying on it.

Keeping things whole
--------------------

The last thing to understand about property in Beleriand is that it
does not last.  Nothing here does.  The Enemy's servants throw fire
and frost about with no thought for what is in your pack, and the
scroll you were saving is a handful of ash the moment a fire-hound
gets a look at it.

The elements are enemies of property.  Fire eats scrolls and staves, acid
armour and ammunition, frost potions, lightning rings, wands, and rods.
Your pack gives a thing some chance to survive; the bare floor gives none.
Resist the element and your goods resist with you, of which more in Of Arms
and the Manner of Fighting.

Which brings the matter back round to Fëanor.  Hold your gear loosely
enough to lose it, and keep the spare in the pack rather than on the
ground, and you will part with less than he did.
