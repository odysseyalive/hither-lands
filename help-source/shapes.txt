{tile:capital:O|O}           f Skin-Changing
            ===============
            "Then Sauron shifted shape, from wolf to serpent, and from
            monster to his own accustomed form..."
            -- The Silmarillion, ch. 19
            Skin-changing is an old art, and not the Enemy's alone.

Sauron wore the wolf and the serpent because they served him, and they
served him well.  But the craft is older than his use of it, and there
are those who put on another skin for reasons he would not recognise.
Some callings learn it out of books.  One people is simply born to it.

What the changing is like is hard to set down, and those who can do it
are not much help, since they mostly shrug.  The nearest a traveller
gets is this.  You are yourself, and then the ground is closer, and the
dark has stopped being dark and has become a set of shapes you can
smell.  Your hands are gone and you do not miss them.  You want to run,
not out of fear, but because running has become the obvious thing to do
with a body.  And underneath all of it you are still there, keeping
accounts, deciding where to go.  That last part is the whole difference
between skin-changing and madness, and it holds.

Who shifts
----------

The art is not open to everyone, and those it is open to come at it by
four different roads.

  Druids take the shapes of beast and stone through their nature
   verses.  Theirs is the widest road and the gentlest, and it ends
   in a bear.

  Necromancers wear the forms of the creatures of night, which are
   quicker and stronger than a Druid's and are not given freely.

  Blackguards learn one savage shape of their own, and one is enough
   for what a Blackguard intends to do with it.

  A Beorning needs no verse at all, for bear-shape is that people's
   birthright, taken at will with '{key:Change shape}', and it
   deepens as they grow.

And any hero whatever, wearing the right dragon-scale armour, may
briefly know what it is to be a dragon.  That is the shortest road of
the lot and the dearest, and nobody who takes it once forgets it.

Wearing another skin
--------------------

Shapes are entered by singing the form's verse with '{key:Sing a song}',
by the Beorning's '{key:Change shape}', or by the power of certain items.
Whichever door you come in by, what waits beyond it is the same.  In a
shape you ARE the beast.  Its blows are yours, and its senses, and its
stealth, and its hide.  Your worn equipment stays on you, changed with
you, and all its virtue still counts, which is the one great mercy of
the art and the reason anyone bothers.

But paws cannot turn pages.  That is the whole of the difficulty, and it
is not a small one.  In beast-form your pack is closed to you, and there
is no reading, no casting, no wands, and no swapping of gear.  You may
eat from the floor and little else.  Attempt anything handier and the
game will ask whether you would care to be a person again first, which
is a courteous way of saying that you have forgotten what you are.

The moment this matters is always the same moment.  Something has gone
wrong, and the answer to it is in your pack, and you are a fox.

Returning
---------

Press '{key:Change shape}' to take your own shape again.  The changing
back costs nothing, and say it plainly that there is no trap
in this art.  Nobody in Beleriand gets stuck as a wolf.

A shape is a tool.  Put it on for the work it is good at, which is
travel, or stealth, or slaughter, and take it off again to think.  The
skin-changers who come to grief are not the ones who shift too often.
They are the ones who forget that they have shifted.

With the Hither Lands tileset your figure upon the map changes with you,
every form having its own sprite, so you need not remember by willpower
alone.

The full particulars of every shape, and there are twenty-two of them,
are set out with numbers and all in Appendix VI, Of Matters Best
Discovered, for those who would rather know than wonder.
